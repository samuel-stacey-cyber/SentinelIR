### 2. Scale and acceptable alternatives

- Needed: `Before CW2 submission`
- Question: What distinguishes missing, partial and adequate responses, and how are alternative correct plans handled?
- Options / trade-offs: Anchored small ordinal scales / points per exact phrase
- My recommendation: Consider 0/1/2 anchors for missing, partial and adequate coverage, with explicit correctness anchors and any penalties defined in advance. Include alternative acceptable responses and unsafe actions; avoid requiring one exact wording or assuming a specific scale is validated.

### 3. Assessor and consistency

- Needed: `Before CW2 submission`
- Question: Who can score plans, and can a subset be independently double-scored?
- Options / trade-offs: Two assessors or checked subset / one assessor with documented limits
- My recommendation: Seek a second assessor for a subset if feasible. Remove condition labels where practical, acknowledge that wording may reveal the condition, calibrate on pilot plans and record agreement before resolving disagreements. Confirm availability rather than promising it.

### 4. Analysis

- Needed: `Before CW2 submission`
- Question: Will the data be paired, what is the primary comparison and what uncertainty can be supported?
- Options / trade-offs: Within-participant differences / independent-group comparison
- My recommendation: Choose analysis after the design is fixed: paired differences for within-participant tasks, independent comparisons for separate groups. Report distributions, effect estimates and uncertainty where justified. Do not treat multiple plans from one student as independent participants; justify any statistical test and its assumptions.

### 5. Pilot and freeze

- Needed: `Before participant research`
- Question: How will you check that the rubric distinguishes good, incomplete and incorrect plans?
- Options / trade-offs: Constructed examples + pilot scoring / revise after seeing final outcomes
- My recommendation: Create example plans, test the anchors, address disagreements and freeze the rubric before scoring final responses. Keep usefulness answers separate; they cannot establish improved technical quality.
